\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented PrivSearch, a privacy-preserving authenticated search framework for hybrid blockchain systems that decouples operational query execution from blockchain trust management, unifying authenticated conjunctive, disjunctive, OR-of-AND, temporal range, bounded fuzzy, and semantic retrieval with real-time replay protection. We established correctness, completeness, replay-resistance, and latency-separation properties through six theorems and four corollaries, backed by an explicit analytical cost model connecting these properties to measured performance.

The prototype was evaluated across 436 Enron-derived and 258 healthcare-derived workloads (281,042 records) with zero verification failures, a weighted mean healthcare latency of 3.3871~ms (80.57\% authentication overhead), 100.00\% replay-rejection accuracy, and 100.00\% WAN-distributed-validation success across validator counts of 2, 4, 7, and 10. Against SeaCQ, PrivSearch showed substantially lower latency at matched conjunctive depths; against TELEX, EAQ, VQL, and MSTDB, it occupies a feature combination --- authenticated verification, expressive post-conjunctive querying, and replay protection --- not jointly covered by prior work.

Key directions for future work include an embedding-based semantic retrieval path, an independently hosted physical deployment study, production-grade hardening of the encrypted-search engine, additional protected clinical corpora, and a dedicated parameter-sensitivity analysis. The current threat model covers the malicious cloud server, replay attacker, stale-proof attacker, and adaptive query adversary, all of which are fully evidenced experimentally; extending the model to \emph{malicious validators} (a single dishonest peer approving incorrect state) and \emph{colluding validators} (a coalition coordinating misleading approvals) requires a threshold-assumption analysis and dedicated adversarial harness experiments that are reserved for future work. These boundaries do not diminish the central result: PrivSearch demonstrates, through formal proofs and extensive empirical validation across two independent corpora, that authenticated blockchain search can be extended well beyond conjunctive-only retrieval while preserving verifiable correctness and practical performance.
